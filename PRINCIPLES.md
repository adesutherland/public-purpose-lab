# Founding principles

## Public purpose before technology

Start with a service problem, the people affected, and the outcome sought.
Technology choices must be justified by that purpose.

## Open by default, private by necessity

Publish code, architecture, synthetic examples, and reusable learning wherever
rights and confidentiality allow. Never publish personal, client-confidential,
NDA-bound, or security-sensitive material.

## People retain accountable control

AI may assist discovery, analysis, drafting, and bounded workflow steps. It does
not silently acquire authority over consequential decisions. Required review,
approval, abstention, refusal, and escalation paths must be explicit.

## Evidence over assertion

Separate ideas, working demonstrations, measured results, and production-ready
claims. Retain provenance, decisions, test evidence, uncertainty, and known
limitations.

## Components own their decisions

Services communicate through explicit commands, events, and interfaces. A
Scenario Director may initiate a demonstration and observe its progress, but it
must not hide business decisions that belong to domain components.

## Privacy and security are behaviour

Zero trust, data minimisation, purpose limitation, disclosure, consent,
retention, and auditability are part of the business design rather than a final
compliance layer.

## Use technology deliberately

No language, platform, or framework is an identity test. Use Rust for the
backend safety and reliability benefits it can provide. Use cREXX assets where
their portability, rule expression, transformation, automation, or integration
value is clear. Record significant choices and their trade-offs.

## Build the smallest decisive experiment

Create the minimum complete path needed to answer a meaningful question. Avoid
both disposable toy demonstrations and premature estates of empty services.
